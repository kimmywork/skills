Nine months after the dike breach

Sediment, salt, and the return of the reed belt

R. Okafor · L. Brandt · M. Chen · T. Aleman
North Inlet Field Station · 18 June 2026

The question

Does the marsh self-heal — or convert to open water?

Two trajectories, one decision: invest in monitoring, or intervene at the breach throat.

Why it matters

One marsh, three public values

Storm buffer

Every hectare of reed belt reduces wave energy before it reaches the dike.

Carbon

Accretion buries carbon; conversion to open water reverses the sink.

Habitat

The reed belt is nursery habitat for the estuary's commercial fishery.

Method

36 fixed transects, sampled monthly

Field

  • 36 transects, three exposure groups
  • Monthly sediment pins
  • Salinity loggers at 3 depths

Remote

  • Drone photogrammetry at 4 cm/px
  • Reed cover classified per transect
  • Storm events dated from tide-gauge records

Nine months · one site · 14 September 2025 – 12 June 2026

Result 1

Sheltered transects crossed the reed threshold; exposed transects did not

3.1 cmSheltered (n=21)
1.4 cmExposed (n=15)
0.6 cmThroat edge (n=6)

Threshold for reed establishment: 2.4 cm. Seven of nine months above threshold on the sheltered side.

Result 2

Interpretation

Self-healing is plausible — but not yet stable.

The breach throat widened during the two largest storms. A nine-month trend cannot distinguish recovery from a pause before the next widening.

Limitations

What this study cannot say

Time

Nine months is shorter than a full storm season and cannot separate recovery from interannual variability.

Space

One site; the throat geometry is not replicated. Transfer to other breaches is an inference.

Mechanism

No belowground biomass or wave-energy measurement at the throat. The mechanism is inferred.

Observation

Reed cover is classified from imagery; observer error is bounded but not eliminated.

Conclusion

Recovery is real, conditional, and worth monitoring

  • Accretion on the sheltered side crossed the reed threshold in 7 of 9 months.
  • Reed cover is limited by wave exposure, not salinity.
  • The throat remains the failure point; the next two storm seasons decide the trajectory.

Recommendation: maintain monthly transects, add wave-energy loggers at the throat, and review after the next storm season.
