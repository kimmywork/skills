# Format Playbooks

Read the matching playbook before you design. Each format has a different job; do not reuse a keynote structure for a working session.

## Sharing session

A low-ceremony session to expose work and invite critique.

- Open with the question or tension, not the agenda.
- Show the work in progress, including what is not settled.
- Give two or three discussion prompts.
- Timebox; capture decisions and owners.
- End with what changes and what happens next.

Failure: a polished presentation that leaves no room for discussion.

## Demo

Show the product doing the thing that matters.

- Script the golden path; preload data; remove setup friction.
- Have a recording as fallback.
- Narrate what the audience should notice, not every click.
- Never debug live; if it breaks, switch to the recording and keep the argument.
- End on the value, not the feature list.

Failure: a feature tour with no scenario.

## Talkshow or panel

A conversation with multiple voices.

- Prepare three stories, three opinions, and three questions.
- Agree handoff cues and who moderates.
- Build on the previous speaker; do not restate.
- Keep answers short enough for the next voice.
- Have a closing line that returns to the throughline.

Failure: four monologues in sequence.

## Workshop

A session that produces an artifact.

- State the objective and the output.
- Give an agenda with timeboxes.
- Use one exercise per block; show the prompt, then get out of the way.
- Capture decisions, open questions, and owners.
- Close by showing the artifact the room produced.

Failure: a lecture with a group exercise bolted on.

## Pitch

A request for a decision or resources.

- Problem, insight, solution, evidence, ask.
- Make the ask specific: amount, decision, next step.
- Show the evidence that the problem is real and the solution can work.
- Name the risk and the mitigation.
- End with the decision you want, not a thank-you slide.

Failure: a product tour with no ask.

## Readout or report

A decision-oriented account of what happened.

- Decision or recommendation first.
- Data and method next.
- Implications and options.
- Next steps and owners.
- Written version: BLUF, stand-alone summary, numbered findings, appendix.

Failure: a chronology of work with no decision.

## Universal

In every format: one takeaway, evidence over adjectives, a clear ask, and a close that fits the time. The format changes the shape, not the standards.
