# Design Philosophy

Read this before choosing a direction or judging one. It gives the stance, the lenses, and the rules for turning values into principles. Use it as a prompt, not a script.

## Stance

Write one short statement before designing: what this product optimizes (speed, trust, calm, density, expression, safety), and what it refuses (dark patterns, attention extraction, complexity without purpose, decoration without function). A system without a stance cannot resolve a disagreement; it can only accumulate preferences.

## Core lenses

Pick two or three. Do not apply all of them.

| Lens | What it protects | Decision test |
|---|---|---|
| Human-centered | Designing from people's goals and context, not from features | Is this decision based on observed need, or on our assumption? |
| Affordance and feedback (Norman) | Predictable action and visible response | Can a new user guess what this does without instructions? |
| Restraint and honesty (Rams) | Less but better; nothing decorative that pretends to be functional | If this element disappears, what information or action is lost? |
| Structure and hierarchy (Swiss grid, Alexander) | Order, grouping, and patterns that resolve real forces | Does the hierarchy survive without color? |
| Inclusion and access | Different abilities, devices, and situations | Does it work at 2× text, with a keyboard, with a screen reader, on a low-end device? |
| Ethics and systems | Persuasion rather than coercion; optimize the whole, not the screen | Would the user consent if they knew everything? What does this change upstream or downstream? |

## Optional lenses

Add one only when it changes a decision: **semiotics** (are signs read as intended across cultures?), **design justice** (who is excluded, who carries the cost?), **emotion and expression** (is the expressive moment serving the moment or distracting from it?).

## Tension pairs

State which side wins in this product, and why:

- simple vs dense;
- consistent vs contextual;
- novel vs familiar;
- expressive vs restrained;
- fast vs safe.

A principle that never forces a trade-off is a slogan.

## From lens to principle

Keep three to seven principles that decide arguments. Each has four parts:

1. **Rule** — what to do.
2. **Rationale** — why it holds.
3. **Trade-off** — what it costs or gives up.
4. **Test case** — a concrete situation where the rule changes a decision.

Write them in the product's own words. Cite a source only when it explains the reasoning; naming a famous designer is not an argument.

## Anti-patterns

- A stance that lists every good quality and refuses nothing.
- A lens used as decoration rather than as a decision test.
- Principles with no trade-off, or rules with no test case.
- Importing another product's philosophy without its constraints.
- Treating accessibility or ethics as a later phase.

## Sources

Dieter Rams; Donald Norman; Christopher Alexander; ISO 9241-210; WCAG 2.2; dark-pattern research; inclusive-design practice. Use them as inputs, not as a canon.
